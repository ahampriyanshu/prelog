# Early Asian and African symbols — provenance and dating

Seven sourced artifact images, added 30 October 2026. These are deliberately labelled as symbols or a royal seal, not invented ancient national flags. The manifest uses exact catalogue IDs; all images are downloaded unchanged, including pre-existing Commons derivatives. SHA-256 is recorded per manifest entry and download metadata is in `public/atlas/symbols/early-asian/download-provenance.json`.

The ranges are **evidence windows for the depicted motif/artifact**, not claims of uninterrupted, uniform territorial use. A museum's approximate object-date range must not be read as exact adoption and abolition years. Select symbols using the viewing year; coarse boundary-snapshot dates are independent.

## Presentation constraints

- Chola seal is an actual transparent PNG and can be shown directly. Keep its complete outer inscription.
- Gupta and Aksum coins are single-side photographs and fit a circular viewport naturally. Gupta's Garuda standard is left of the ruler; do not crop it away. Aksum is a small source image (202×205), appropriate for a small map imprint, not a large detailed plate.
- Sarnath is tall: preserve the lions **and** wheel. Center-only circular cropping loses the heads. Use contained sizing or a deliberate viewport that retains those features; do not replace it with the modern state-emblem drawing.
- Yuan: the full inscription supplies its meaning as imperial authority. Do not use a center-only crop that chops the inscription or presents the animal mask alone as a Yuan state emblem.
- Ming: a central red-field detail can serve as a textile-motif imprint. Avoid the 18th-century outer border.
- Qing: a central-body detail retains the dragons and avoids later neck/sleeve bands. It remains explicitly a robe motif.

## Gaps intentionally left open

No Maratha, Mughal, unified-Mongol or Mali asset was added in this bounded pass. Accessible modern SVGs often imply standard shapes/colors or long date ranges that the historical evidence does not establish. Mughal lion-and-sun standards are documented in Edward Terry's account and 17th-century paintings, but a separately licensed faithful period image with appropriate display framing was not completed here. A late Yuan paiza was not relabelled as Genghis Khan's emblem. No object was assigned to Mali simply because it is West African.

The Kulottunga Chola coin on Commons has an unreviewed GODL permission claim, and the common “Uttama” coin has disputed ruler attribution; the securely identified LACMA royal seal was used instead. The British Museum/Ismoon Ezana cross image was not used because the file's claimed self-license and museum-image source are not cleanly reconciled. The CNG permission chain is explicit.

Seven local assets were visually inspected. All seven IDs exist, dates lie within their catalogue lifespans, image bytes have valid signatures, and manifest/download hashes agree. These checks do not establish modern territorial boundaries or continuous use of a motif across every region of an empire.

## Renderer viewport metadata

Every entry includes `imageWidth` and `imageHeight`, read from the downloaded file with macOS `sips`, and `mapPresentation: "contain"`. This retains complete coins, the Chola seal, Sarnath capital and Yuan pass inside an inscribed medallion.

The two textiles additionally specify normalized `mapCrop: [x, y, width, height]`. These are viewport instructions over the unchanged original image, not modified source pixels:

- **Ming (478×900):** source-pixel rectangle **x=70, y=380, width=340, height=360**. This central red field contains the dominant dragon motif and excludes the later outer border.
- **Qing (4000×3001):** source-pixel rectangle **x=1400, y=1500, width=1200, height=900**. This lower central body field shows dragon imagery and longevity characters, below the later diagonal neck band and away from the later sleeve bands. It deliberately does not attempt to include the entire upper dragon at the expense of including a later collar.

Apply a robe viewport before contained medallion sizing. If the renderer does not support `mapCrop`, show the complete artifact with its dating note instead of implying that the later parts are original-period insignia; preferably withhold the robe map imprint until the viewport is supported.
