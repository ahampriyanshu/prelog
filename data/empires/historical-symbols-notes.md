Historical symbol asset notes

Scope: 14 unchanged Wikimedia Commons SVG originals, attached to 10 historical dossier IDs in 18 non-overlapping, dated entries. The manifest is keyed by current catalogue IDs. These are verified historical flag designs; the Byzantine image is explicitly labelled a reconstruction of a documented naval ensign. No generic flags were invented for ancient states.

Use the entry only when start <= selected year <= end. Do not extend a symbol over the full dossier period. The UI has annual resolution, so transition years are handled conservatively: some are excluded rather than imply one design applied for every day. ModernOnly=false means a historically appropriate entry, not a claim that the SVG drawing itself is an ancient object.

Provenance: source-metadata.json contains the Commons API licensing/author metadata captured during retrieval. asset-provenance.json records the original download URL, source page, SHA-256, byte count, and unchanged status of every file. CC BY-SA 3.0 applies to the Byzantine, Spanish, and 1955 Soviet drawings; the remaining files are Commons public-domain entries. Preserve the manifest attribution and license links in the UI or an accessible credits panel. No SVG paths or colours have been edited.

Independent dating evidence
- Union Flag: present design dates to 1 January 1801. Commons source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_the_United_Kingdom_(1-2).svg ; contemporary 1801 flag illustration: https://www.history.navy.mil/our-collections/photography/numerical-list-of-images/nhhc-series/nh-series/NH-66000/NH-66218.html
- France: the Elysée documents restoration of the tricolour in July 1830: https://www.elysee.fr/en/french-presidency/the-french-flag . The lighter 1974 rendering has its own dated Commons file. National/metropolitan flags represent the colonial umbrella; no claim is made that every colony had the same local ensign.
- Russia: government sources distinguish the 1858 black-yellow-white state colours, the 1883 ceremonial/civil order, and the final 1896 national designation of white-blue-red. We use 1858–1882 and 1896–1917 to avoid the disputed intermediate status: https://rostransnadzor.gov.ru/news/2919 and https://www.prlib.ru/news/1301206 . The 1896 file is a period-specific vector rather than the modern Russian SVG.
- Portugal: the blue-white flag was introduced by the liberal regency in 1830, with national adoption after civil-war victory in 1834. The republic sanctioned its new flag in June 1911. Sources: https://ccmuseus.azores.gov.pt/index.php/Detail/objects/154972 , https://www.museu.presidencia.pt/pt/conhecer/simbolos-nacionais/bandeira-nacional/ , https://www.presidencia.pt/en-en/president-of-the-republic/the-presidency/national-symbols/ . Manifest excludes the contested 1830–1833 and transition year 1910.
- Spain: the 1785 design was initially naval; its extension to the army dates to 1843. Separate entries label the naval and later general uses. The crown-bearing graphic is not assigned to republican periods: https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/lang/en/espana/statesymbols/paginas/index.aspx .
- Byzantine: Commons documents the reconstructed imperial naval ensign from the fourteenth-century Book of All Kingdoms and Pseudo-Kodinos, not a universal national flag: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Byzantine_imperial_flag,_14th_century.svg . Display starts c.1350, not 1261, because the cited attestation is mid-fourteenth-century.
- Qing: the retrieved rectangular Yellow Dragon design is explicitly 1889–1912: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_China_(1889–1912).svg . No retroactive use for seventeenth/eighteenth-century Qing.
- Ottoman: the retrieved late five-pointed-star design is explicitly 1844–1922: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_the_Ottoman_Empire_(1844–1922).svg . Earlier Ottoman flags are not assumed.
- Soviet: separate original vectors for the 1936 design and 1955 construction scheme. The latter is limited to 1955–1979; the 1980 change is not modeled. Commons source pages and exact URLs are in the manifest/provenance.
- Ethiopia: the source file describes the imperial Lion of Judah flag, with the caption identifying Haile Selassie’s reign. Use is therefore limited to 1930–1935 and 1941–1973; the 1936–1940 Italian occupation and the 1974 transition are excluded. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_Ethiopia_(1897–1974).svg . This deliberately does not assert that its exact lion drawing was uniformly used throughout 1897–1930.

Coverage gaps
- No asset supplied for the other 33 curated dossiers: Akkadian, Neo-Assyrian, Achaemenid, Alexander, Roman Republic, Roman Empire, Rashidun, Umayyad, Abbasid, Holy Roman Empire, Aztec, Inca, Mauryan, Gupta, Chola, Delhi Sultanate, Mughal, Maratha, Sikh, Qin, Han, Tang, Song, Yuan, Ming, united Mongol, Khmer, Srivijaya, Majapahit, Mali, Songhai, Aksum, Zulu. A blank symbol is preferable to a modern or imagined national flag. These need separately verified archaeological motifs, documented standards, or historical drawings with reliable dates and reusable licenses.
- Earlier/later uncovered portions of the ten represented dossiers also remain blank. In particular, no Palaiologan flag is asserted for 1261–1349, no modern SPQR/eagle is assigned to Rome, and no modern Khanda/Nishan Sahib is asserted for the Sikh Empire.
- Colonial flags here are metropolitan flags with explicit labels, not inferred territorial unions or claims about local administration.
